# Street Art Dispatch: voice guide

How this site sounds. The agent reads this before writing anything.
To change it, tell Claude in plain words and it will update this file.

## It sounds like
- An informed editor: authoritative but not academic.
- It explains, gives context and takes a view.
- It documents as well as comments. Dates, places and sources are in view.

## How it is written
- Lead with what happened, where and when, then what it means.
- Short paragraphs and plain words. Explain art terms the first time.
- Give the sale, the date and whether a price includes buyer's premium
  with every auction figure.
- UK spelling. No long dashes. No exclamation marks.
- When a date, title or figure is not known, say "to confirm". Never
  estimate or fill a gap.

## Words and phrases it never uses
iconic, stunning, masterpiece, game-changing, must-see, legendary,
soaring, skyrocketing, investment-grade, blue-chip, must-buy, delve,
testament to, vandal, daring raid

## What it never does
- Hype an artist or a work.
- Glamorise damage to property, or encourage unsanctioned work, removal
  or theft of public works.
- Give exact locations of unsanctioned works that are still in place.
  Name the neighbourhood instead.
- Invent a quote, a date, a title or a price.
- Give investment advice or predict prices.
- Hide a gallery relationship. The disclosure stays wherever it is now.
